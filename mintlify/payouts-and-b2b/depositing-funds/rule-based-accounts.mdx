A rule-based account is an additional account number for an account holder, with a routing rule attached. Every payment that settles into it is attributed to that account and then swept to the rule's destination. Issue one account per payer, invoice, or use case, and you can reconcile incoming payments by the account they arrived at instead of matching on remittance text.

Rule-based accounts never hold a balance. Funds that land in one are always moved on, either to the rule's destination or, if the sweep can't complete, to the account holder's own internal account in the same currency. The exception is an EU account whose payout fails after the conversion: those funds wait for an authorized transfer (see [Funds a sweep couldn't deliver](#funds-a-sweep-couldnt-deliver)).

## Supported currencies

| Currency | What payers send to |
|----------|---------------------|
| `USD` | A dedicated US bank account and routing number |
| `USDC` | A dedicated deposit address on each supported network for your platform (for example, Solana or Base) |
| `USDT` | A dedicated deposit address on each supported network for your platform (for example, Tron or Ethereum) |

## Prerequisites

- The account holder already has an internal account in the same currency. Funds go there when a sweep can't be completed.
- The account holder is verified. For a platform-owned account, your platform's own customer record must be verified.
- The sweep destination exists. It can be an external account or another internal account, but not another rule-based account.

## Create a rule-based account

Call `POST /internal-accounts` with `type: RULE_BASED`, the currency, and a `sweepRule`. Omit `customerId` to create an account owned by your platform.

<Tip>
  Always send an `Idempotency-Key`. Each request issues a new account number or address that can't be reversed, so the key is what keeps a retry from creating a second one.
</Tip>

## Configure the sweep rule

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| `destination.accountId` | Where funds are swept. It can be in a different currency, in which case each sweep is converted at the prevailing rate. |
| `destination.paymentRail` | The rail used for an external destination. Omit it to pick a rail automatically for each sweep. Not accepted for internal destinations. |
| `purposeOfPayment` | Applied to each sweep. Some destination countries require it. |
| `description` | Recorded on each sweep for your own records. Not sent to the recipient. |
| `remittanceInformation` | Sent with each sweep to the recipient, for example in the ACH addenda or wire OBI. Printable ASCII only. |
| `platformFeeOverride` | Fee terms for every sweep this rule drives. Omit it to use your platform's configured fees. |

When you read the account, `sweepRule.minimumAmount` and `sweepRule.maximumAmount` show the range the route to the destination can carry, in the rule-based account's currency.

## Track sweeps

Each sweep appears as a transaction with `ruleBasedAccountId` set to the rule-based account that received the deposit. Its `source` describes the depositor when it's known.

A sweep that can't be completed fails with one of these reasons, and the funds move to the account holder's own internal account in the same currency:

- `SWEEP_AMOUNT_OUT_OF_RANGE`: The amount is below `minimumAmount` or above `maximumAmount`.
- `SWEEP_QUOTE_FAILED`: Grid couldn't price the sweep.

A sweep that was priced and then failed on the way to settlement reports `QUOTE_EXECUTION_FAILED`, the same as any other payment. Include it alongside the `SWEEP_*` reasons when you monitor for failed sweeps.

To list an account holder's rule-based accounts, filter internal accounts with `type=RULE_BASED`.

## EU customers

For customers in a region where Strong Customer Authentication (SCA) is required, a rule-based account converts every deposit and sends the result to its destination. The sweep runs without any further action once the account holder has authorized it.

| Currency | What payers send to | Supported destinations |
|----------|---------------------|------------------------|
| `EUR` | A dedicated IBAN | A crypto external account owned by the account holder (for example, a USDC wallet), or the account holder's own `USDC` or `BTC` internal account |
| `USDC` | A dedicated deposit address | An `EUR` external bank account owned by the account holder, or their own `EUR` internal account |
| `BTC` | A dedicated deposit address | An `EUR` external bank account owned by the account holder, or their own `EUR` internal account |

A same-currency destination isn't supported, and neither are `remittanceInformation` or `purposeOfPayment` on the sweep rule. A customer can hold up to 10 rule-based accounts.

### Authorize the account

The create request returns the account in `PENDING` with an `scaChallenge`. The account holder authorizes it once, and later sweeps run without further authorization:

1. Make sure the customer has an active SCA login session. A missing session returns `409 SCA_SESSION_REQUIRED`.
2. For an external destination, start beneficiary trust with `POST /customers/external-accounts/{externalAccountId}/trust`, then submit the customer's proof to `POST /customers/external-accounts/{externalAccountId}/trust/confirm`. Check that the response contains `trusted: true` before creating the account. An untrusted destination returns `409 BENEFICIARY_NOT_TRUSTED`.
3. Call `POST /internal-accounts` with an `Idempotency-Key`. Pass `scaFactor` to choose `SMS_OTP` (default), `TOTP`, or `PASSKEY`.
4. Call `POST /internal-accounts/{id}/authorize` with the customer's proof. The account becomes `ACTIVE` and Grid sends `INTERNAL_ACCOUNT.STATUS_UPDATED`.

If the challenge expires, repeat the create request with the same `Idempotency-Key` to receive a new challenge. An account that is never authorized moves to `FAILED`.

### Funds a sweep couldn't deliver

If a payout is refused after the conversion (for example, by the destination bank or a compliance check), the converted funds stay with the account holder. They are listed in the account's `strandedBalances`. Move them to the account holder's own internal account in that currency with `POST /internal-accounts/{id}/stranded-funds/transfer`, which returns a quote to authorize like any other SCA-protected transfer.

## Test in sandbox

In sandbox, account numbers and addresses are generated locally. Call `POST /sandbox/internal-accounts/{accountId}/fund` on a rule-based account to simulate a settled deposit. It triggers the sweep exactly as a real deposit would. Fund a very small amount to test the `SWEEP_AMOUNT_OUT_OF_RANGE` path.
